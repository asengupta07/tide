Five products for Tokyo,
one of them is the winner.

A working dossier for ETHGlobal Tokyo 2026: the research frontier in EVM DeFi, five buildable products that sit on it, how each one stacks sponsor prizes without looking bolted-on, and the whitepaper-plus-video playbook that turns a hack into a win.

Prepared Friday 25 Sep 20267 sponsors, $52,500 on the boardContinuity-only prizes excluded from stacks

How we win

Hackathon judging at ETHGlobal is a two-stage filter. Stage one is a sponsor engineer reading your README and code at 2 a.m., asking "did they actually use my thing, and did it matter?" Stage two is a three-minute live judging slot where a tired human decides whether your project is memorable. Our two aces map exactly onto those stages: the whitepaper wins stage one (it proves depth and makes the sponsor's integration look load-bearing), and the demo video wins stage two (it makes the project memorable before we even open our mouths).

Three rules follow from that.

  1. The mechanism is the product. Pick one piece of unpublished-in-code research and make it the reason the project exists. Every sponsor integration must be something that mechanism needs, not something we needed to hit a prize.
  2. Three sponsors deep beats six sponsors shallow. Several prize texts on this page literally say "not a cosmetic add-on". Judges from the sponsor whose integration is thin will score you down, and it costs you the sponsor whose integration was strong because the project reads as prize-farming.
  3. Every requirement is a checkbox in the README. Sponsors here ask for specific artifacts: FEEDBACK.md and a feedback form (Uniswap), an integration debrief (World), a README pointing at the API call lines with 3 to 5 lines of API feedback (Intercepta), proper commit history (1inch), a live demo link on Sepolia (ENS). Missing one is an automatic drop from that sponsor's shortlist.

Recommendation. Build Product A, Tide. It carries the freshest research (two 2026 arXiv papers with no implementation anywhere), it fits the two highest-value technical tracks (1inch Aqua and Uniswap v4) with the same math library, and its parameter-governance story gives ENSv2 and World ID for Agents a real job. Realistic prize exposure is $24,500 to $26,500 across four to six prizes. Keep C as the fallback if the SwapVM work stalls on Saturday afternoon: it has the lowest technical risk and the highest sponsor count.

Research frontier: published, unbuilt

The filter here is strict: mechanisms with a paper in 2026 (or a hot open problem) and no production implementation. Anything that already ships (am-AMM at Bunni, batch-auction FM-AMM at CoW and Angstrom, CCA at Uniswap, Arrakis's LVR-rebate POC) is treated as a component or as prior art to differentiate from, never as the headline.

1. Partially Active AMMs (PA-AMM)

arXiv 2602.09887, Sunghun Ko, February 2026. The pool splits reserves into an active part that trades and a passive part that does not. At the top of every block the split is redone so that active reserves are a λ-fraction of total, λ ∈ (0, 1]. Only the active fraction is exposed to the block's first arbitrageur, so loss-versus-rebalancing (LVR) drops relative to a plain CFMM. The trade-off is drift: asset weights inside the pool deviate from the invariant's target, and the paper solves an index-tracking style optimisation to pick λ. The author lists fees and gas as limitations for future work, which is the tell that nobody has put it on-chain.

2. Hybrid Liquidity-Collateral Pool (HLCP)

arXiv 2605.19267, Kim and Park, May 2026. A stylised architecture that partially decouples execution quality from active risk exposure: an N-scaled virtual invariant gives traders slippage as if the pool were N times deeper, backed by a collateral buffer that only engages when price deviates past a threshold. Duopoly analysis shows adoption is stable and improves on constant-product for all participants. Unimplemented.

3. Uniform-loss AMMs for prediction markets

arXiv 2607.17428, Moallemi, Robinson (Paradigm) and Zhu, July 2026. Extends the LVR framework to prediction-market AMMs. A "uniform" AMM has instantaneous LVR proportional to pool value and independent of the current price. For a broad class of win-martingales (processes converging to 0 or 1 at a fixed resolution time) there exists a pricing function with uniform LVR, and liquidity can be scheduled over time to hit a prescribed expected cumulative loss. This is the successor to pm-AMM. No hook exists.

4. Time-priority auctions and their centralisation

Two threads. The theory (arXiv 2512.10094, "Auctioning Time to Mitigate Latency Races") models single-winner time-priority auctions as implemented by Arbitrum's Timeboost. The empirics (arXiv 2509.22143) analyse 48.5 million express-lane transactions and 494k auctions between January and April 2026 and find three entities winning 99.74% of auctions. Open problem: an application-level time-priority market that recycles proceeds to LPs and resists that centralisation.

5. Agent commerce is mostly not agentic yet

By late April 2026 Coinbase reported 165 million x402 transactions across roughly 69,000 agents against about $50 million cumulative volume, roughly thirty cents per transaction. TRM's strict test (facilitator-broadcast, sub-dollar, varying price, registered in ERC-8004 or paying several sellers) puts the share of screened x402 commerce that looks agentic between 0.6% and 7.5%. Identity-bound, screened, policy-constrained agent payments are still a green field, which is why World, ENS, Intercepta and Curvegrid all wrote agent prizes.

Sponsor primitives that make these buildable

  • 1inch Aqua / SwapVM. Aqua is the registry, custody and accounting layer: inventory stays in the maker's wallet, strategies are shipped and docked, and the Shared Liquidity Ratio lets one real balance back several positions with no leverage or debt because fills are asynchronous and each fill is capped by tokens actually present. SwapVM is the execution engine: a strategy is a byte program of [opcode][argsLength][args] run inside AquaSwapVMRouter during quote() and swap(). On the deployed router the executable subset is Controls, XYCSwap, XYCConcentrate, Decay, Fee, PeggedSwap and Extruction. Third-party opcode slots are reserved per family bank in OpcodeList.sol, and the track explicitly permits redeploying a modified router. Swaps are currently executed only by verified 1inch Resolvers holding a soulbound KycNFT, so demos impersonate a resolver on a fork. Aqua's "one balance, many positions, pulled only at fill time" is structurally the PA-AMM passive reserve and the HLCP collateral buffer already.
  • Uniswap CCA and v4 hooks. CCA generalises the uniform-price auction to continuous time; bids carry a max price and a spend, are non-withdrawable while in range, and clear block by block. Optional validation hooks run custom logic before a bid is accepted (allowlists, rate limits, arbitrary rules) and may re-enter the auction. After graduation lbpInitializationParams() hands clearing price, tokens cleared and net proceeds to an ILBPInitializer that seeds a v4 pool. v4 hooks give beforeSwap with BeforeSwapDelta (full custom swap math), dynamic fees, and beforeAddLiquidity (JIT control).
  • ENSv2 on Sepolia. Hierarchical registries; wildcard resolution off a parent's resolver; deployable subname registries; Enhanced Access Control (shared role-based permissions across registries and resolvers, fine enough to let an account edit only specific text records); Permissioned Resolvers so a subname owns its data; record aliasing and namespace aliasing; expiring, revocable, non-transferable and "forever" subname setups. ENSIP-25 (agent registry name verification) and ENSIP-26 (agent text records) plus an agent-native CLI.
  • World ID for Agents (dev env). An OpenID Connect IdP: the relationship scope is a sector, the private identifier arrives as a pairwise sub claim, and "fresh authentication" (RFC 9470 step-up) brings the human into the flow for high-value actions. Agents bind issuer plus subject to a grant and receive credentials for APIs or an MCP server; the sandbox exposes an MCP server at /mcp that walks your coding agent through the integration.
  • World IDKit. Credentials: Proof of Human, Passport/NFC (ICAO 9303), Selfie Check (now with a Sybil score). Judged on choosing the minimum credential for the trust moment.
  • Intercepta. One API call screens a wallet, contract, token, signature or transaction: sanctions and AML exposure, scam and phishing attribution, drainer approvals, fake tokens, simulated balance changes. Endpoints: Quick Scan Address, Deep Scan Address, Scan Token, Scan Message (the x402 payment authorisation). x402 uses EIP-3009 transferWithAuthorization for USDC and Permit2 for other tokens, so Scan Message runs on that authorisation before the agent signs. Risk data is mainnet; test addresses with known risks are pinned in their Discord.
Product A, recommended build

Tide

Partially-active liquidity with a collateral buffer, shipped as a SwapVM program on 1inch Aqua and as a Uniswap v4 hook from one shared math library, with a manager agent that can only move the activeness parameter after a fresh human check.

$24.5k+prize exposure: 1inch, Uniswap, ENS, World Agents, Curvegrid
Highresearch novelty: two 2026 papers, zero implementations
Mediumtechnical risk: SwapVM opcode work is the long pole

Thesis

Passive LPs lose to the first arbitrageur of every block. Every shipped answer either taxes the arbitrageur (dynamic fees, am-AMM), batches away their edge (FM-AMM, Angstrom) or rebates them (Arrakis). PA-AMM is the first design that simply shows the arbitrageur less: only λ of the pool is quotable at the top of the block, and the rest sits passive until the next block. HLCP adds the mirror trick for the uninformed trader: quote against an N-scaled virtual curve so retail sees deep liquidity, and let a buffer absorb the difference when price actually moves. Aqua is the natural home because a maker's wallet balance is already a passive reserve that is only pulled at fill time; the v4 hook version proves the mechanism is venue-agnostic.

Mechanism and the maths that goes in the whitepaper

Setup follows Milionis, Moallemi, Roughgarden and Zhang. Price Pt follows a geometric Brownian motion with volatility σ. For a CFMM with pool value V(P), instantaneous LVR is

(1)ℓ(P) = ½ σ² P² |x′(P)|,   and for constant product   ℓ = (σ²/8) · V(P).

Proposition 1 (activeness scales LVR). Let a PA-AMM with total equity E expose active reserves A = λE on a constant-product active curve at the top of each block, with the passive remainder untouched within the block. Then per-block expected LVR is λ times the plain CFMM's, to first order in the block-length Δ:

(2)𝔼[LVRPA] = λ · (σ²/8) · E · Δ + O(Δ3/2).

Proof sketch. Within a block the arbitrageur faces a constant-product curve of value λE; apply (1) to that curve. The passive reserves do not move within the block, so they contribute no rebalancing loss; the re-split at the next block is a book-keeping transfer between the two parts at the same marginal price and creates no arbitrage. The correction term comes from the gap between the active curve's marginal price and the true price after the re-split, which the paper models as a stationary gap process. State the gap dynamics exactly as in Section 3 of arXiv 2602.09887 and cite it; do not redo it.

Proposition 2 (the cost). Because only λ of reserves rebalance, the pool's asset weights drift from the invariant's target. With target weight θ and realised weight wt, the paper's objective is

(3)minλ   𝔼[LVRPA(λ)] + κ · 𝔼[(wt − θ)²]

with κ the LP's tracking-error aversion. Solve numerically for the demo (a 30-line Python script in research/), plot the frontier, and let the manager agent read λ* off it.

Proposition 3 (virtual depth with a buffer, HLCP). Quote uninformed flow against the invariant x·y = N²k instead of k. Slippage for a trade of size q falls from ≈ q/x to ≈ q/(N x). The pool cannot actually deliver N-scaled reserves, so a buffer B tops up the active reserves whenever |ln(Ppool/Pref)| > δ. In Aqua the buffer is the maker's remaining wallet balance under the Shared Liquidity Ratio; in v4 it is a hook-held ERC-6909 claim. State the solvency condition as an invariant: active + buffer ≥ the maximum the virtual curve can be asked to deliver before the next re-split, i.e. the buffer must cover the worst-case trade at the deviation threshold.

Put (2) and the solvency invariant in the paper as the two things you prove, (3) as the thing you solve, and the paper's own gap dynamics as the thing you cite. Then include a one-page simulation: 10,000 Monte-Carlo paths, σ = 60% annualised, 12-second blocks, λ ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1}, LP wealth versus HODL. That single chart is what the 1inch and Uniswap judges will screenshot.

Product B, research purist

Fair-loss markets

A Uniswap v4 hook implementing the uniform-loss prediction-market AMM from Paradigm's July 2026 paper, with one-human-one-position enforced by World ID and every market a self-owning ENSv2 subname.

$21.5kUniswap, World IDKit, ENS, Curvegrid Dash
Highestpaper is two months old; authors are Paradigm's
Mediumpricing function needs a numerical solver on-chain

Thesis

Prediction-market AMMs descend from market scoring rules where a subsidiser eats a bounded worst-case loss. The literature bounded the total; nobody controlled where and when the loss lands. A market maker at price 0.95 with two days to resolution is bleeding at a completely different rate than one at 0.50 with a month left. The uniform AMM makes instantaneous LVR a constant fraction of pool value regardless of price, and the dynamic extension lets you schedule liquidity to hit a target cumulative subsidy. That is a product: a market creator sets a budget and a resolution date, and the hook spends the budget evenly.

Mechanism

A binary outcome token has price pt ∈ (0,1) following a win-martingale that resolves at T. With Gaussian-score dynamics (the pm-AMM setting), dp = φ(Φ−1(p)) / √(T−t) dW. For a pricing function with reserves function x(p), instantaneous LVR is ½ σeff²(p,t) p² |x′(p)|. The uniform condition is

(4)½ σeff²(p) p² |x′(p)| = c · V(p)   for all p,

an ODE in x(p) whose solution the paper characterises (Theorem 11 relies on Sturm-Liouville results; cite, do not reprove). The dynamic part: choose liquidity L(t) so that ∫0T 𝔼[ℓt] dt equals the creator's budget, which for the uniform AMM reduces to a closed-form schedule because ℓt is proportional to V.

On-chain you do not solve an ODE. Precompute x(p) on a 256-point grid off-chain, store it as a packed table in the hook, interpolate in beforeSwap, and prove in the paper that piecewise-linear interpolation at that resolution bounds the uniformity error below the swap fee. That bound is the second proposition of the whitepaper.

Sponsor roles

  • Uniswap. Full custom curve via BeforeSwapDelta; time-varying liquidity via the hook's own accounting; beforeAddLiquidity restricted to the market creator so nobody JITs the subsidy.
  • World IDKit, Proof of Human. Trust moment: a market's subsidy is a scarce benefit, and a sybil can farm it by holding both sides across wallets. Proof of Human is the minimum credential (no passport needed; you do not need to know who someone is, only that they are one person). Verify the proof server-side, mint a per-market nullifier, and refuse a second position. Alternative path shown: user with no credential is shown a read-only market.
  • ENSv2. markets.eth runs a subname registry; each market is btc-100k-dec.markets.eth with a Permissioned Resolver holding question, resolution, oracle, budget, and outcome tokens as yes.btc-100k-dec.markets.eth via record aliasing. Markets are expiring names that revoke at resolution.
  • Curvegrid Dashboard. The LVR-schedule versus realised-loss chart is the product's own UI.

Product C, safest stack

Human-gated CCA

A Continuous Clearing Auction validation hook that enforces one-human-one-allocation with per-human caps, plus a bidding agent that must get fresh human approval above a threshold and screens the auction's recipients through Intercepta before it signs.

$23.5kUniswap, World (one prize), ENS, Intercepta, Curvegrid Agent
LowCCA is shipped; novelty is in the gating and the agent
Loweverything is a thin integration on audited contracts

Thesis

CCA solved price discovery but not who gets allocation: a whale with fifty wallets still eats a fair launch. Aztec's launch bolted on a ZK passport module; a general, permissionless version that any issuer can attach is missing. The trust moment is crisp: before a bid enters the clearing set, the auction needs to know the bidder is one human under their cap. Proof of Human is the minimum credential; Passport/NFC is the upgrade for jurisdiction-gated sales, and you show both as a config switch.

Mechanism

The hook implements IValidationHook.validate(maxPrice, amount, owner, sender, hookData). hookData carries the World ID proof; the hook verifies it on-chain against the World ID router, derives the nullifier, and maintains spent[nullifier]. Cap logic: spent + amount ≤ cap, otherwise revert, which by the CCA spec prevents bid submission. Because the CCA's validation hook may re-enter the auction, the proposition in the whitepaper is a small one: the cap invariant holds under re-entrancy because the nullifier map is updated before any external call (checks-effects-interactions, stated formally with the re-entry sequence).

The paper's second contribution is allocation fairness: with per-human caps, the uniform clearing price is bounded below by the price at which capped demand alone meets supply, so a single actor cannot pin the clearing price. One lemma, one figure comparing clearing paths with and without caps on simulated bid streams.

Sponsor roles

Product D, mechanism-design flex

Express-lane hook

A per-pool time-priority auction inside a Uniswap v4 hook: bidders buy the first-swap slot of each block, proceeds stream to in-range LPs, and a consecutive-win cap plus identity binding attacks the 99.74% centralisation Timeboost showed.

$15kUniswap, ENS, Intercepta, Curvegrid Agent
Highdirect answer to a 2026 empirical finding
Medium-highauction settlement inside a hook is subtle

Thesis

Chain-level express lanes (Timeboost) internalise MEV for the sequencer and, empirically, for three entities. am-AMM auctions the right to set fees; this auctions the right to go first, which is the actual scarce good in the LVR story, and pays LPs rather than the sequencer. The whitepaper's contribution is the anti-concentration rule: a bidder may not win more than m of any n consecutive blocks, and bids are bound to an ENS agent name so the rule is per identity rather than per address.

Mechanism

Sealed-bid via commit in block b, reveal in b+1, slot in b+2; or a simpler ascending on-chain auction using CCA's continuous-clearing logic per block. The hook's beforeSwap checks slot[block.number]: if set and sender != winner and the winner has not yet swapped, revert. Proceeds accrue to the hook as ERC-6909 and are distributed to positions in range at the winning swap's tick, like Angstrom's tick-based rewards. Proposition: with the m-of-n cap, the maximum long-run share any single identity can capture is m/n, and total LP revenue is at least the second-highest valuation per block (Vickrey argument if you use second-price reveal).

Timeboost's data is your motivation slide; state the numbers and cite the paper. The theory paper (arXiv 2512.10094) gives you the framing of "reallocating surplus from dissipation to revenue"; you reallocate it to LPs.

Sponsor roles

  • Uniswap. The hook, plus an example bidder bot in the repo.
  • ENSv2. Bidders register as agent subnames under lane.eth; the cap is keyed by name, and Enhanced Access Control lets the pool operator suspend a name. ENSIP-25 verification ties the bidding address to the name.
  • Intercepta. The bidder agent pays its winning bid over x402 to the hook's settlement endpoint; Quick Scan on the counterparty and Scan Message on the authorisation before signing. A pinned-risk hook address gets blocked.
  • Curvegrid AI Agent. The bidder is an on-chain monitoring agent that estimates per-block arb value and bids a fraction.

Risk

The commit-reveal across blocks means the demo needs a fork with block mining under your control and a narrative that survives the "what if the winner does not show" question (answer: slot lapses, bid is forfeit to LPs). No World prize fits naturally; do not force one.

Product E, agent-first

Policy firewall

An agent treasury that provisions Aqua positions and pays for data over x402, where every outbound payment is screened by Intercepta, every spend policy lives as ENSv2 text records the human alone can write, and every policy escalation requires fresh World ID authentication.

$21.5kWorld Agents, 1inch, ENS, Intercepta, Curvegrid Agent
Mediummarket-structure novelty is low; the composition is the story
Low-mediummostly integration; 1inch part must be real SwapVM

Thesis

The agent-payment stack has three layers and each one hands the same question to the next: x402 moves money, ERC-8004 says who the agent is, and something has to say what the agent is allowed to do. Today that "something" is a prompt or an operator's server, which a counterparty cannot inspect. The firewall makes policy an on-chain object (ENS records under EAC), makes counterparty risk an on-chain decision (Intercepta verdict before the signature), and makes exceptions a human event (World step-up). The whitepaper is short and formal: a policy language, its evaluation semantics, and a theorem that no payment exceeding the policy can be signed without a fresh human token.

Mechanism

Policy π = (daily cap, per-counterparty cap, allowlist of ENS names, max Intercepta risk score, categories). Evaluation: for a proposed payment (to, amt, token, auth), compute the Intercepta verdict r; if r.score ≤ π.max and caps hold → sign; if r.score ≤ π.max but a cap fails → request World fresh auth, sign only on a validated token whose sub matches the policy owner; else → refuse and log. The 1inch part is not decorative if the agent's job is to keep Aqua positions funded: it ships and docks SwapVM strategies (a real program, e.g. a concentrated XYC with Decay for inventory skew) and pays for the price feed it uses over x402.

Why not the pick

It is the most obviously stackable design and the World Agents judges will love it, but the 1inch integration is the weakest of the five and the Uniswap prize is absent. If the team splits into two, this is the second project: reuse Tide's agent, ENS and World code and add the payment path.

Whitepaper playbook

Judges skim. A hackathon whitepaper is not a journal paper; it is a six-to-ten page document whose job is to make a sponsor engineer think "these people understand the problem better than I do" within ninety seconds. Structure it so the first two pages do all the work.

Structure that works

  1. Abstract (120 words). Problem, mechanism, one quantitative claim, what was built.
  2. The problem in one figure. For Tide: LP wealth vs HODL under a plain CFMM. For D: the Timeboost concentration numbers as a bar. The figure must be legible at thumbnail size because it will be screenshotted into a judging spreadsheet.
  3. Model and notation. Half a page. Reuse the LVR literature's notation exactly; sponsors' research teams know it.
  4. Mechanism. Pseudocode and a state diagram. Instruction order or hook callback order stated explicitly.
  5. Two propositions with proofs, one problem solved numerically. Proofs are short and honest about what is cited versus what is new. A hackathon judge is more impressed by a correct one-paragraph proof than a two-page one with a hole.
  6. Simulation. Monte-Carlo with parameters stated, code in research/, one chart, one table.
  7. Implementation. Contract map, gas table (before and after), test coverage line count, the exact opcode slots or hook flags used.
  8. Sponsor integration rationale. One paragraph per sponsor answering "why was this needed", written so it can be pasted into the sponsor's feedback file.
  9. Limitations and future work. Honest and specific. Judges read this section to check whether you know what you did not do.
  10. References. arXiv numbers, repo commit hashes.

Production notes

  • Write it in Typst or LaTeX from Saturday morning, not Sunday. The paper drives the demo script, not the other way round.
  • Every equation that appears in the video must appear in the paper with the same numbering.
  • Put the PDF at the repo root as WHITEPAPER.pdf and link it in the first line of the README and on the showcase page. Add a two-minute "paper walkthrough" as a chapter of the demo video for judges who prefer to listen.
  • Include a one-page appendix that maps every sponsor qualification requirement to a file path and a line number. This is the single highest-leverage page in the document.

Demo video playbook

ETHGlobal videos are watched on a laptop at 1.5x by a judge who has forty to get through. The first ten seconds decide whether they watch the rest; the last ten decide whether they remember it.

Shape

  • 0 to 10 s: the hook. One animated chart or one sentence that states the problem as a loss. Not the team, not the logo.
  • 10 to 50 s: the idea. Whiteboard or motion graphic with one equation. Say the paper's arXiv number out loud; it signals depth without a slide.
  • 50 s to 2:00: it works. Real terminal, real fork, real events. Show balances change. Show the phone. Show the failure path; sponsors asked for it and most teams skip it.
  • 2:00 to 2:30: the stack. Thirty seconds, one sentence per sponsor, in order of integration depth.
  • 2:30 to 2:45: the close. Repo, whitepaper, the team, and the one number to remember (e.g. "λ = 0.5 cut LVR by 48% in simulation").

Craft

  • Record terminal sessions with a large font and a light theme; dark terminals compress badly on YouTube.
  • Voice over recorded in one take per section, then cut; do not read the script live over the screen capture.
  • Charts from the same Python script as the paper, exported as video via matplotlib animation, so the video and the paper agree to the pixel.
  • Chapters in the description: judges jump. Put "failure path" as its own chapter so sponsors can verify their requirement in five seconds.
  • Total length under three minutes. If it needs more, the product needs less.
